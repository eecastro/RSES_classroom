Reading these numbers

  • These are class results for teaching and discussion. They describe groups, never a student, and who chooses to take the scale moves the numbers.
  • Scores run 0–30 (each item 0–3, items 2, 5, 6, 8 and 9 reverse-scored). Add 10 for the 1–4 scoring some textbooks use.
  • The 15–25 “typical range” is a rule of thumb, not a clinical cutoff. The scale has no official cutoffs and is not a screener.
  • If the share below 15 looks high for a section, the dashboard can’t tell you who. A general reminder to the whole class about counseling resources is the right response.
  • Reliability (Cronbach’s alpha) around .80–.90 is typical for this scale in published studies. Item-total correlation shows how well each item tracks the other nine; low values flag items students may be reading differently.
  • Semesters come from the date each response was saved: Fall runs from the start of the school year through December, Spring from January on.
